c) I'll divide the model into four groups: attention projections (1.01 TFLOPs, 28.62% of total), attention score/value matmuls (0.32 TFLOPs, 9.16% of total), FFN (2.02 TFLOPs, 57.53% of total), and LM head (0.16 TFLOPs, 4.68% of total). Therefore, the feed forward network requires the most FLOPs, because the FFN contains twice the number of learnable parameters (60.22% of the total) as the multi-head self attention component and the number of FLOPs is directly proportional to learnable parameters times the context length for weight matmuls.

As the model size increases, the attention projections and FFN consume proportionally more FLOPs due to scaling with O(d_model^2), while the attention score/value matmuls and LM head consume proportionally fewer FLOPs due to only scaling linearly with d_model.
e) If we 16x the context length to 16,384, then attention projections take 16.11 TFLOPs or 12.06% of total, attention score/value matmuls take 82.46 TFLOPs or 61.73% of total, FFN takes 32.37 TFLOPs or 24.24% of total, and LM head takes 2.63 TFLOPs or 1.97% of the total. The dramatic rise in proportion of FLOPs dedicated to attention score/value matmuls can be explained by scaling with O(context_length^2), while the other three components only scale linearly with context size, and total FLOPs increase ~38× (from 3.52 to 133.58 TFLOPs), far more than the 16× increase in context length.
